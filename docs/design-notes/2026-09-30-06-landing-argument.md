# Design note 06: the argument the landing page makes, 2026-09-30

Written before landing page C. Mihai asked for deeper thinking on how the page shows what the
product is, how it works and why it is better. No reference material was looked at; decision
0002 stands (Mihai chose to keep it after considering two of his own past deliverables).

## Who reads the page

Two people, one need.

1. A delivery lead or partner at a consultancy, mid-engagement, with a list of 60 to 120
   requirements that the client's experts must confirm before the next steering meeting.
2. A head of product or PMO lead with a roadmap, a catalogue or a platform requirement list
   that internal experts must check before budget is committed.

Both have done this before with a spreadsheet, an email thread and a workshop. Both know how it
ends: three versions of the file, comments in cells, half the people answering, no record of who
agreed to what, and a synthesis written by hand the night before.

## What the page has to prove, in order

1. What it is, in one sentence, with the product visible in the first screen.
2. That it replaces something specific and painful, not "surveys" in general.
3. How it works, in three steps, each ending in something you hold in your hand.
4. That respondents will finish it. Every validation fails at this step.
5. That reasons come back, not votes. This is the core difference from forms and boards.
6. What comes back: six concrete outputs, each shown as a real fragment.
7. That the numbers are trustworthy and the AI is grounded.
8. Who it is for, in their words.
9. How it compares with what they use now, without spin.
10. Price, and one action.

## Advantages, and how each is shown rather than claimed

| Advantage | Shown by |
|---|---|
| Readable at 80 items | The phone, one item per screen, with the area and "2 of 6" visible |
| Reasons, not votes | A mandatory reason box in the hero loop; a band of real-looking reasons |
| People finish it | No account, autosave, resume, one link; the sign-off screen |
| A record exists | The sign-off record with name, time and confidence |
| Answers become decisions | Actions with the responses they cite; an uncited action never shown |
| Numbers you can defend | "Every number matches the export" and a live dashboard fragment |
| Cheaper than the alternatives | A comparison row with public prices from the plan |

## What is not on the page

Customer logos, quotes and outcome numbers: none exist. They stay as marked placeholders or are
left out. No claims about time saved. No feature planned for R2 or R3 except in the pricing
table, where the plan lists them.

screens. Results. Who it is for. Compared with what you use now. Pricing. Closing. Footer.
