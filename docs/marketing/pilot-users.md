# The first two real users

Goal: two people, outside the project, who run a real validation with five or more
respondents in the next fourteen days, give feedback through the Feedback page (E17-1) and
one 30-minute call each. Not product managers at software companies if it can be helped: an
operations, IT or project person at a company of 10 to 250 people, or a consultant who runs
requirement workshops. The messages are in docs/marketing/outreach-drafts.md.

## The offer

A year of Pro at no cost once the plans exist, a 30-minute call with Mihai, fixes for what
hurts them within the week, and their name nowhere unless they ask. Nothing is sold; the
product is free while it is built.

## Where they come from, in order

1. Mihai's own network. The fastest and the only one with a known reply rate. Mihai writes
   five names in docs/marketing/log.md (ex-colleagues, clients of Alerty, people who told him
   about a tool choice in the last year). Claude writes one message per name, in the person's
   language; Mihai sends them from LinkedIn or Claude sends them from his Gmail on his go.
   Expected: two of five say yes to a call.
2. Product Makers (productmakers.ro, "1,700+ on Slack"). Mihai joins, posts in #intros as the
   site asks, and asks the organisers whether a feedback request is welcome and where. If
   yes, the post in outreach-drafts.md asks for one person who has a list to get agreed this
   month. Expected: one or two replies; the pool is product managers, so the pilot found here
   is a feedback source more than a buyer.
3. LinkedIn, twenty people. Claude builds the list by title and company size (operations
   manager, head of operations, IT manager, project lead, at 10 to 250 people, Romania first)
   from LinkedIn's own filters, which Mihai runs; the message references one thing the
   person posted. Expected reply rate unknown; the log records it.
4. Consultants, five. Implementation partners for CRM, ERP or HR tools in Romania, found by
   web search by Claude; one email each, the report as the pitch.

## What a pilot does

1. Signs in, imports one real list (the template on the landing page if theirs is not ready).
2. Publishes a link and sends it to at least five people who know the work.
3. Reads the Results and the to-do list, exports the CSV or the PDF.
4. Sends three reports through the Feedback page: what was wrong, what was missing, what
   they would pay for.
5. The call: thirty minutes, Mihai asks, Claude has written the questions (below) and reads
   the notes Mihai writes in docs/marketing/log.md.

## The call

Ten questions, in order; the first five matter most.
1. What did you use last time you had to get a list like this agreed? How long did it take?
2. Walk me through what you did in SMEsay from the first screen. Where did you stop?
3. What did your respondents say? Did anyone not answer, and why?
4. Which number on the Results page did you look at first? Did you trust it?
5. What would you have needed to show this to your manager or your client?
6. What was missing? What was in the way?
7. If this cost EUR 49 a month, who at your company would pay, and from which budget?
8. Who else do you know who has to get a list agreed this quarter?
9. May we quote one sentence from you on the landing page, with your first name and role?
10. May we write to you when the next version is out?

## Done

Both pilots have published, five or more respondents each have submitted, the three fixes
they named are merged, their quotes (if given) are in docs/copy/landing.md with their
permission recorded in docs/marketing/log.md.
